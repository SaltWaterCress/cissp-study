Study Guide

All 8 Domains — Deep Dive

Based on the Sybex CISSP Study Guide and official ISC² exam outline. Select a domain to study.

D1

Security & Risk Management

The largest domain. Covers foundational security concepts, risk frameworks, legal/regulatory compliance, personnel security, and business continuity. Think like a CISO managing organizational risk — not a technician.

▲ 16% of Exam (Updated 2024)

🔒 CIA Triad & Security Concepts

+

Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability — the three pillars of information security.

FoundationalAlways Tested
Key Points
  • Confidentiality — prevent unauthorized disclosure. Controls: encryption, access control, classification.
  • Integrity — prevent unauthorized modification. Controls: hashing, digital signatures, version control.
  • Availability — ensure authorized access when needed. Controls: redundancy, backups, DDoS protection.
  • Non-repudiation — cannot deny performing an action. Achieved via digital signatures.
  • Authenticity — verifying identity is genuine. Beyond just authentication.
CISSP questions often ask which CIA component is violated. Disclosure = Confidentiality. Modification = Integrity. Denial of access = Availability.

⚖️ Security Governance

+

Aligning security with business strategy through policies, standards, and oversight.

ManagementHigh Priority
Policy Hierarchy
  • Policy — high-level management statements of intent. Mandatory.
  • Standards — specific mandatory requirements supporting policies.
  • Guidelines — recommended but optional best practices.
  • Procedures — step-by-step instructions to implement policies.
Key Concepts
  • Due Care — doing what a reasonable person would do (minimum standard).
  • Due Diligence — ongoing research to maintain due care.
  • Board oversight — security must align with business mission and goals.
Security function exists to SUPPORT the business — not the other way around. Always choose the answer that aligns security with business objectives.

📊 Risk Management

+

Identifying, assessing and responding to risks using qualitative or quantitative methods.

QuantitativeCore Topic
Risk Formula
  • Risk = Threat × Vulnerability × Asset Value
  • Threat — anything that can exploit a vulnerability (attacker, fire, flood).
  • Vulnerability — weakness that can be exploited.
  • Risk Appetite — how much risk the org is willing to accept.
  • Risk Tolerance — acceptable variation around risk appetite.
Risk Responses
  • Accept — acknowledge and live with the risk.
  • Transfer — shift to third party (insurance, contracts).
  • Avoid — eliminate the activity causing the risk.
  • Mitigate — reduce likelihood or impact with controls.
Qualitative = subjective (High/Med/Low). Quantitative = dollar values (ALE, SLE). Most orgs use hybrid. Know when to use each.

🧮 Risk Calculations (Quantitative)

+

Mathematical formulas to calculate financial impact of risks. Must memorize for exam.

MathMemorize
Core Formulas
  • SLE = Asset Value × Exposure Factor (EF) — loss from single event.
  • ALE = SLE × ARO — annual expected loss.
  • ARO — Annual Rate of Occurrence (0.5 = once every 2 years).
  • EF — % of asset destroyed in one event (expressed as 0–1).
  • Countermeasure Value = ALE(before) – ALE(after) – Annual Cost
Example
  • Server value: $500K. Fire destroys 40% (EF=0.4). Occurs 0.5×/yr.
  • SLE = $500K × 0.4 = $200,000
  • ALE = $200K × 0.5 = $100,000/year
If a control costs less than ALE(before) – ALE(after), it's worth implementing. This is the ROI justification for security spending.

📜 Legal, Regulations & Compliance

+

Laws, regulations, and IP frameworks that govern information security globally.

Regulatory
Key Regulations
  • GDPR — EU privacy law. 72-hr breach notification. Fines up to 4% global revenue.
  • HIPAA — US healthcare data (PHI). Privacy + Security Rules.
  • SOX — Public companies, financial reporting integrity.
  • PCI-DSS — Payment card data. 12 requirements. Any org processing cards.
  • FERPA — Student education records (US).
Types of Laws
  • Criminal — government prosecutes, beyond reasonable doubt, imprisonment.
  • Civil (Tort) — private disputes, preponderance of evidence, monetary damages.
  • Administrative — government agencies, compliance fines.
Intellectual Property
  • Patent — inventions, 20 years protection.
  • Copyright — creative works, life + 70 years.
  • Trademark — brand identity, renewable indefinitely.
  • Trade Secret — protected indefinitely as long as kept secret.
Know which regulation applies to which industry. Healthcare = HIPAA. Finance = GLBA. Public companies = SOX. Any payment card = PCI-DSS.

🏗️ Security Frameworks

+

Structured approaches to organizing and implementing security programs.

FrameworkHigh Priority
Key Frameworks
  • NIST CSF — Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover. Risk-based, voluntary.
  • ISO 27001 — ISMS certification standard (requirements). Auditable.
  • ISO 27002 — Code of practice / implementation guide for 27001.
  • COBIT — IT governance and management framework.
  • ITIL — IT service management best practices.
Framework vs Standard
  • Framework — flexible guidance, adaptable (NIST CSF). Voluntary.
  • Standard — specific mandatory requirements (ISO 27001, PCI-DSS).
NIST CSF is the most commonly referenced in CISSP scenarios. Know the 5 functions in order: Identify → Protect → Detect → Respond → Recover.

👥 Personnel Security

+

People-focused controls from hiring through termination to reduce insider threats.

HROperational
Pre-Employment
  • Background checks — criminal, credit, employment verification.
  • NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement) — signed before access granted.
  • Employment agreements — AUP, security responsibilities.
During Employment
  • Separation of Duties — no single person controls entire process.
  • Least Privilege — minimum access needed for job function.
  • Mandatory Vacation — detects fraud (someone must cover your role).
  • Job Rotation — cross-training and fraud detection.
Termination
  • Friendly — exit interview, return assets, disable accounts.
  • Hostile — HR present, escort out, disable access BEFORE notification.
For hostile terminations: disable access FIRST, then inform the employee. Sequence matters in exam questions.

🏢 Business Continuity (BCP)

+

Planning to maintain business operations during and after a significant disruption.

BCP/DRHigh Priority
BCP vs DRP
  • BCP — maintains business operations. Long-term, business-wide scope.
  • DRP — restores IT systems. Tactical, IT-focused. Subset of BCP.
Key Terms
  • BIA (Business Impact Analysis) — identifies critical functions and their dependencies.
  • MTD — Maximum Tolerable Downtime. Business limit before unacceptable damage.
  • RTO — Recovery Time Objective. How fast must systems be restored. Must be < MTD.
  • RPO — Recovery Point Objective. How much data loss is acceptable (backup frequency).
  • WRT — Work Recovery Time = MTD – RTO.
Recovery Sites
  • Hot Site — ready immediately, most expensive.
  • Warm Site — hours to activate, medium cost.
  • Cold Site — days/weeks to activate, cheapest.
RTO must always be less than MTD. If RTO > MTD, the business cannot survive. BIA is always performed FIRST before writing BCP.

🔑 Security Awareness Training

+

Building a security-conscious workforce through education, training, and awareness.

People
Awareness vs Training vs Education
  • Awareness — general security mindset. Posters, reminders. For all employees.
  • Training — specific skills for a role. Hands-on. For practitioners.
  • Education — deep theoretical knowledge. Degrees, certifications. For specialists.
Social Engineering Attacks
  • Phishing — fraudulent email to steal credentials.
  • Vishing — voice/phone-based phishing.
  • Pretexting — fabricated scenario to manipulate victim.
  • Tailgating/Piggybacking — physical access by following authorized person.
Awareness training is the most cost-effective security control. People are always the weakest link. Training programs need regular updates and testing (e.g. simulated phishing).

🤝 Third-Party Risk

+

Managing security risks from vendors, suppliers, and service providers.

Supply Chain2024 Added
Key Controls
  • Vendor due diligence — security assessments before onboarding.
  • SLAs — Service Level Agreements define security expectations.
  • Right-to-audit clause — contractual right to audit vendor security.
  • SCRM — Supply Chain Risk Management program.
Assessment Methods
  • On-site assessment — visit vendor facilities.
  • Document review — policies, procedures, certifications (SOC 2, ISO 27001).
  • Process/policy review — examine vendor security practices.
Your organization is responsible for data even when a third party handles it. You can transfer risk via contracts but cannot transfer accountability.

🔬 Threat Intelligence

+

Understanding threat actors, their capabilities, and how to model and share threat data.

Threat
Threat Actor Types
  • Nation-state — most sophisticated, well-funded, long-term campaigns (APT).
  • Organized crime — financially motivated, ransomware, fraud.
  • Hacktivists — ideologically motivated, DDoS, defacement.
  • Insider threats — most dangerous, already have access.
  • Script kiddies — low skill, use existing tools.
Threat Modeling
  • STRIDE — Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Info Disclosure, DoS, Elevation of Privilege.
  • PASTA — Process for Attack Simulation and Threat Analysis.
  • STIX/TAXII — standards for sharing threat intelligence.
APT (Advanced Persistent Threat) = nation-state level. Characteristics: stealthy, long-term, targeted, well-resourced. Insider threats cause the most financial damage per incident.

🛡️ Ethics

+

ISC² Code of Ethics — the ethical framework every CISSP must follow.

EthicsAlways Tested
Code of Ethics Canons (in priority order)
  • 1. Protect society — common good, public trust and confidence.
  • 2. Act honorably — honestly, justly, responsibly, and legally.
  • 3. Provide competent service — to principals (employers, clients).
  • 4. Advance the profession — through education, sharing knowledge.
Application
  • When canons conflict, higher-numbered items yield to lower-numbered ones.
  • Society always comes before employer interest.
  • Must report illegal activity even if employer disagrees.
Classic exam scenario: your employer asks you to cover up a breach. Ethics Canon 1 (protect society) overrides Canon 3 (serve employer). Always choose to protect the public.
D2

Asset Security

Focuses on classifying information and supporting assets, determining ownership, handling data throughout its lifecycle, and applying appropriate protection measures. Resource protection and data management are central themes.

10% of Exam

🏷️ Data Classification

+

Labeling data based on its sensitivity to determine appropriate protection levels.

ClassificationCore
Government Levels
  • Top Secret — grave damage to national security if disclosed.
  • Secret — serious damage to national security.
  • Confidential — damage to national security.
  • Unclassified — no damage expected.
Commercial Levels
  • Confidential/Proprietary — most sensitive business data.
  • Private — personal employee/customer data.
  • Sensitive — requires extra precautions.
  • Public — freely shareable.
The Data Owner sets the classification. The Data Custodian implements the controls. Never confuse these two roles.

👤 Data Ownership

+

Roles and responsibilities for managing data throughout its lifecycle.

RolesAlways Tested
Key Roles
  • Data Owner — senior business manager. Accountable. Sets classification and requirements.
  • Data Custodian — IT department. Implements technical controls. Not accountable.
  • Data Processor (GDPR) — processes data on behalf of the controller.
  • Data Controller (GDPR) — determines purpose and means of processing.
  • User — accesses data per approved policy.
Owner = accountability. Custodian = responsibility for implementation. A manager who owns a database is the owner; the DBA who manages backups is the custodian.

♻️ Data Lifecycle

+

The stages data passes through from creation to permanent destruction.

Lifecycle
Lifecycle Stages
  • Create — data is originated or received.
  • Store — data is saved (encryption at rest required).
  • Use — data is accessed and processed.
  • Share — data is transmitted (encryption in transit required).
  • Archive — long-term storage with retention policies.
  • Destroy — permanent, verifiable deletion.
Destruction Methods (NIST SP 800-88)
  • Clear — overwrite with new data. Protects against simple recovery.
  • Purge — degaussing or secure erase. Protects against lab attacks.
  • Destroy — physical destruction (shred, incinerate). Highest assurance.
Data remanence = residual data after deletion. Degaussing only works on magnetic media, NOT SSDs. For SSDs use crypto-shredding or physical destruction.

🔒 Data States

+

The three states data exists in and the appropriate protection for each.

Data StatesCore
Three States
  • Data at Rest — stored on disk, database, backup. Controls: AES encryption, access controls, full disk encryption.
  • Data in Transit — moving across a network. Controls: TLS 1.3, VPN/IPSec, HTTPS, SFTP.
  • Data in Use — actively in RAM/CPU. Controls: DRM, trusted execution environments, memory encryption.
Data in Use is the hardest to protect. This is why cold boot attacks (dumping RAM) are so dangerous — they target data in use.

🗑️ Data Retention & Destruction

+

Policies for how long data must be kept and how it must be destroyed.

Destruction
Retention Drivers
  • Legal requirements — regulations mandate minimum retention periods.
  • Business requirements — operational and historical needs.
  • eDiscovery / Legal Hold — preserve data relevant to litigation.
Destruction Methods
  • Overwriting — DoD 5220.22-M (7 passes for HDDs). Not effective for SSDs.
  • Degaussing — magnetic field erasure. Only for magnetic media.
  • Physical destruction — shredding, incineration. Highest assurance.
  • Crypto-shredding — delete encryption keys. Best for cloud/SSD.
Crypto-shredding is the recommended method for cloud-hosted data since physical destruction isn't possible. Destroy the keys = destroy the data.

🔐 Privacy Protection

+

Protecting personal information in compliance with privacy regulations globally.

PrivacyGDPR
Key Definitions
  • PII — Personally Identifiable Information (name, SSN, email, IP address).
  • PHI — Protected Health Information (medical records, diagnoses).
  • NPI — Non-Public Personal Information (financial data — GLBA).
GDPR Key Principles
  • Data minimization — collect only what's necessary.
  • Purpose limitation — use only for stated purpose.
  • Right to erasure — "right to be forgotten".
  • Data portability — users can export their data.
  • Privacy by design — build privacy in from the start.
GDPR applies to ANY organization processing EU residents' data, regardless of where the org is located. Fines: up to €20M or 4% of global annual revenue.

📦 Asset Management

+

Tracking and managing hardware, software, and data assets throughout their lifecycle.

Assets
Key Concepts
  • Asset inventory — complete list of all hardware, software, data assets.
  • CMDB — Configuration Management Database. Central register of all IT assets and relationships.
  • Asset valuation — quantify value for risk calculations (used in SLE/ALE).
  • Tangible assets — servers, laptops, cables.
  • Intangible assets — IP, data, reputation, brand.
You cannot protect what you don't know you have. Asset inventory is the foundation of all security programs. NIST CSF starts with "Identify" for this reason.

☁️ Cloud Data Security

+

Securing data in cloud environments using appropriate tools and shared responsibility.

Cloud
Key Controls
  • Shared Responsibility Model — cloud provider secures infrastructure; customer secures data and access.
  • CASB — Cloud Access Security Broker. Visibility and control over cloud usage.
  • DLP — Data Loss Prevention. Prevents sensitive data leaving the org.
  • Data sovereignty — data subject to laws of country where it's stored.
Cloud Service Models
  • IaaS — customer responsible for OS, apps, data.
  • PaaS — customer responsible for apps and data.
  • SaaS — customer responsible for data and access only.
In SaaS, the provider handles almost everything — but YOU are still responsible for who has access and what data you put in. Shared responsibility never disappears.
D3

Security Architecture & Engineering

Covers secure design principles, security models, cryptography, physical security, and vulnerability assessment across computing architectures. The most technically deep domain.

13% of Exam

🏛️ Secure Design Principles

+

Core principles every security architect must apply when designing systems.

PrinciplesCore
Key Principles
  • Least Privilege — minimum access needed. Reduce blast radius of compromise.
  • Defense in Depth — multiple layers of security controls.
  • Fail Secure — default to denied state on failure.
  • Separation of Privilege — require multiple conditions to grant access.
  • Economy of Mechanism — keep security simple; complexity = risk.
  • Open Design — security shouldn't depend on secrecy of design (Kerckhoffs's principle).
  • Zero Trust — never trust, always verify. Assume breach mentality.
Defense in Depth = multiple independent layers. If one fails, others remain. Like an onion — not a single wall.

🔐 Security Models

+

Formal frameworks defining rules for access control and data protection.

ModelsAlways Tested
Confidentiality Models
  • Bell-LaPadula — No Read Up, No Write Down. Protects confidentiality. Military use.
Integrity Models
  • Biba — No Read Down, No Write Up. Protects integrity. Opposite of BLP.
  • Clark-Wilson — Integrity via well-formed transactions (CDI, TP, IVP). Commercial use.
Other Models
  • Brewer-Nash (Chinese Wall) — prevents conflict of interest. Legal/financial use.
  • State Machine — must be secure in all possible states. Foundation for others.
BLP = Confidentiality. Biba = Integrity. Remember: they are opposite in their rules. Bell-LaPadula prevents leaks DOWN; Biba prevents corruption from BELOW.

🖥️ Security Evaluation Criteria

+

Standards for evaluating and certifying the security of IT products.

Evaluation
Common Criteria (ISO 15408)
  • EAL1 — Functionally tested (lowest assurance).
  • EAL2 — Structurally tested.
  • EAL3 — Methodically tested and checked.
  • EAL4 — Highest commercially feasible. Most common target.
  • EAL5–6 — Semiformally designed and verified.
  • EAL7 — Formally verified (mathematical proof). Highest assurance.
Other Standards
  • TCSEC (Orange Book) — US DoD. D (minimal) → C1/C2 → B1/B2/B3 → A1.
  • Protection Profile — security requirements for a product category.
  • Security Target — vendor's claim of how their product meets a PP.
EAL4 is the highest level routinely achieved commercially. EAL7 is theoretical — almost nothing achieves it in practice.

🔑 Cryptography Fundamentals

+

Symmetric, asymmetric, and hashing algorithms — the backbone of security.

CryptoCore
Symmetric (same key)
  • AES — 128/192/256-bit. Block cipher. Current gold standard. FIPS 197.
  • 3DES — 112–168-bit. Being phased out. Slow.
  • Blowfish/Twofish — variable key up to 448-bit.
  • RC4 — stream cipher. BROKEN. Used in WEP (why WEP is broken).
Asymmetric (key pairs)
  • RSA — based on prime factorization. 2048+ bits recommended.
  • ECC — smaller keys, same security. 256-bit ECC ≈ 3072-bit RSA.
  • Diffie-Hellman — key EXCHANGE only, not encryption.
  • DSA — digital signatures only, NOT encryption.
Hash Functions
  • MD5 — 128-bit. BROKEN. Never use for security.
  • SHA-1 — 160-bit. WEAK. Deprecated.
  • SHA-256/512 — Current standard. Use these.
  • HMAC — Hash + secret key = integrity AND authentication.
Hybrid systems = asymmetric for key exchange, symmetric for bulk data. This is how TLS works. RSA for the handshake, AES for the session.

📜 PKI & Digital Certificates

+

Infrastructure for managing digital certificates and public/private key pairs.

PKIHigh Priority
PKI Components
  • CA (Certificate Authority) — issues and signs certificates. Root of trust.
  • RA (Registration Authority) — verifies identity before CA issues cert.
  • X.509 — certificate format standard used in TLS/HTTPS.
  • CRL — Certificate Revocation List. Batch, periodic. Latency issue.
  • OCSP — Online Certificate Status Protocol. Real-time revocation check.
Digital Signatures
  • Signing — sender encrypts hash with PRIVATE key.
  • Verification — receiver decrypts with sender's PUBLIC key.
  • Encryption — encrypt with recipient's PUBLIC key; decrypt with PRIVATE.
  • Key Escrow — copy of private key held by third party for recovery.
Signatures use your PRIVATE key (only you can sign). Encryption uses recipient's PUBLIC key (anyone can encrypt to them). Memorize this — it's always tested.

🏗️ System Security Architecture

+

The technical design of secure computing environments and protection mechanisms.

Architecture
Key Concepts
  • TCB (Trusted Computing Base) — all hardware/software/firmware providing security. Smaller = more secure.
  • Reference Monitor — abstract concept; mediates all access between subjects and objects.
  • Security Kernel — hardware/software implementing the reference monitor.
  • Ring Architecture — Ring 0 (kernel/most trusted) → Ring 3 (user apps/least trusted).
Processing Modes
  • Dedicated — all users have same clearance, same need-to-know.
  • System High — all users cleared but not all need-to-know.
  • Compartmented — formal access approval required for each compartment.
  • Multi-level — different users have different clearance levels simultaneously.
The TCB is like the security boundary of a system. Everything inside TCB is trusted. The smaller the TCB, the smaller the attack surface.

🛡️ Physical Security

+

Protecting physical assets, facilities, and personnel from physical threats.

Physical
Defense Layers (outside in)
  • Perimeter — fences, walls, lighting, CCTV, guards.
  • Outer — building exterior, badge readers, reception.
  • Inner — internal zones, mantraps, biometrics.
  • Target — server room, vault, data center.
Key Controls
  • Mantrap — two-door entry, only one opens at a time. Prevents tailgating.
  • CPTED — Crime Prevention Through Environmental Design. Design spaces to deter crime.
  • Bollards — concrete posts to stop vehicles.
  • FM-200 / Halon — clean agent fire suppression for data centers (won't damage equipment).
Physical security is the first and last line of defense. No technical control matters if an attacker has physical access to your servers.

☁️ Cloud Security Architecture

+

Security considerations for cloud deployments, virtualization, and containers.

Cloud2024 Focus
Virtualization Threats
  • VM Escape — attacker breaks out of VM to access hypervisor or other VMs.
  • Hypervisor attack — compromise of the hypervisor = access to all VMs.
  • VM sprawl — uncontrolled proliferation of VMs.
Cloud-Native Security
  • Container security — image scanning, least-privilege containers, read-only filesystems.
  • Zero Trust Architecture — verify every request; no implicit trust based on network location.
  • Serverless — no persistent server to patch; risk shifts to function code and permissions.
Zero Trust = "never trust, always verify." Every access request must be authenticated and authorized regardless of network location. Key for modern cloud environments.

🔌 IoT & Embedded Systems

+

Security challenges for constrained devices, industrial systems, and embedded hardware.

IoTICS/SCADA
IoT Challenges
  • Constrained resources — limited CPU/RAM makes full encryption difficult.
  • Firmware security — signed firmware, OTA update integrity verification.
  • Default credentials — major risk; always change default passwords.
  • Physical access — devices often deployed in unsecured locations.
ICS/SCADA
  • Purdue Model — hierarchical model for ICS network segmentation.
  • Air gap — physical separation between OT and IT networks.
  • Availability priority — ICS prioritizes availability over confidentiality (opposite of traditional IT).
In ICS/SCADA environments, AVAILABILITY is the top priority — a power plant can't go offline for patching. This flips the CIA triad priority order.

⚠️ Common Vulnerabilities

+

Software and hardware vulnerabilities that attackers commonly exploit.

Vulnerabilities
Key Vulnerability Types
  • Buffer overflow — writing beyond allocated memory. Can execute arbitrary code.
  • Race condition / TOC/TOU — Time of Check vs Time of Use. Exploit timing gap.
  • Covert channel — unintended communication path. Storage (data) or Timing (behavior).
  • Side-channel attack — exploit physical characteristics (power, timing, EM emissions).
  • Rootkit — hides malware by subverting the OS kernel.
TOC/TOU (Time of Check / Time of Use) = attacker changes a resource AFTER it's been validated but BEFORE it's used. Classic race condition exploit.
D4

Communication & Network Security

Design and protect network architecture, understand protocols, implement secure communications, and protect against network attacks. Know the OSI model cold and which security measures apply at each layer.

13% of Exam

🌐 OSI & TCP/IP Models

+

Layered networking models that define how data moves across networks.

ModelsAlways Tested
OSI 7 Layers (top to bottom)
  • 7 Application — HTTP, DNS, SMTP, FTP. User-facing.
  • 6 Presentation — Encryption, compression, format conversion.
  • 5 Session — Session establishment, NetBIOS.
  • 4 Transport — TCP (reliable), UDP (fast). Ports live here.
  • 3 Network — IP, ICMP, routing. Logical addressing.
  • 2 Data Link — MAC addresses, ARP, switches, frames.
  • 1 Physical — Cables, hubs, bits.
Mnemonic (top to bottom): All People Seem To Need Data Processing. Firewalls typically operate at Layer 3–4. WAFs at Layer 7. IDS/IPS at Layer 3–7.

🔒 Network Security Protocols

+

Protocols that provide security for network communications.

ProtocolsCore
Secure Protocols
  • TLS 1.3 — current standard. Replaced SSL. Eliminated weak ciphers.
  • SSH — replaces Telnet. Encrypted shell access. Port 22.
  • HTTPS — HTTP over TLS. Port 443.
  • SFTP/SCP — secure file transfer over SSH.
  • DNSSEC — signs DNS records. Prevents spoofing. Does NOT encrypt DNS.
  • S/MIME — email encryption + signing. Uses X.509 certificates.
Deprecated/Insecure
  • SSL, TLS 1.0/1.1 — deprecated. Do not use.
  • Telnet — plaintext. Replace with SSH.
  • FTP — plaintext. Replace with SFTP.
  • HTTP — no encryption. Replace with HTTPS.
Always choose the encrypted alternative. TLS 1.2 is acceptable but TLS 1.3 is preferred. Anything older than TLS 1.2 is considered broken.

DMZ (3-legged firewall), segmentation, VLANs, microsegmentation. East-west vs north-south traffic. Zero Trust Network Access (ZTNA). Software Defined Networking (SDN) and NFV.

ArchitectureHigh Priority

🛡️ IDS/IPS

+

Systems that monitor network traffic to detect and optionally block attacks.

IDS/IPS
IDS vs IPS
  • IDS — detects and alerts only. Passive. Out-of-band (no impact on traffic).
  • IPS — detects and blocks. Active. Inline deployment (can impact traffic).
  • NIDS — monitors network traffic. HIDS — monitors a single host.
Detection Methods
  • Signature-based — matches known attack patterns. Fast, low false positives. Can't detect zero-days.
  • Anomaly-based — detects deviations from baseline. Can find new attacks. High false positives.
  • Behavioral — monitors behavior patterns over time.
False positive = legitimate traffic flagged as attack (annoying). False negative = real attack missed (dangerous). IPS inline = can block but also can break legitimate traffic.

⚡ Network Attacks

+

Common attacks targeting network infrastructure and communications.

Attacks
Attack Types & Mitigations
  • DoS/DDoS — overwhelm resources. Mitigate: rate limiting, CDN, scrubbing centers.
  • ARP Poisoning — associate attacker MAC with legitimate IP. Mitigate: dynamic ARP inspection.
  • DNS Hijacking — redirect DNS queries. Mitigate: DNSSEC.
  • MITM — intercept communications. Mitigate: TLS, mutual authentication.
  • Replay Attack — capture and resend auth data. Mitigate: timestamps, nonces.
  • Session Hijacking — steal session token. Mitigate: HTTPS, secure cookies, re-auth.
For every attack on the exam, know the countermeasure. CISSP tests "what would you do" not just "what is this attack."

📞 Voice & Multimedia Security

+

Security for VoIP and unified communications systems.

VoIP
VoIP Security
  • SRTP — Secure Real-time Transport Protocol. Encrypts voice/video data.
  • SIP vulnerabilities — toll fraud, call interception, DoS attacks.
  • VLAN separation — put voice traffic on dedicated VLAN, separate from data.
  • Vishing — voice phishing. Social engineering via phone calls.
Always separate voice and data VLANs. VoIP without SRTP = unencrypted calls that can be intercepted and replayed.

Identity & Access Management (IAM)

Controls how users, devices, and services are identified, authenticated, and granted access. The first line of defense. Understand the full IAM lifecycle and federation technologies.

13% of Exam

🪪 Identification vs Authentication vs Authorization

+

The three fundamental steps of access control — claiming, proving, and granting.

Core ConceptsAlways Tested
The Four Steps
  • Identification — claiming an identity. Typing a username. No proof yet.
  • Authentication — proving the identity. Password, token, biometric.
  • Authorization — what actions are permitted. ACLs, roles, policies.
  • Accountability — logging all actions for audit trail. Non-repudiation.
All four must be present for a complete access control system. Missing accountability = no audit trail = inability to investigate incidents.

🔐 Authentication Factors

+

The categories of evidence used to prove identity.

AuthenticationCore
Three Primary Factors
  • Something you Know — password, PIN, passphrase, security question.
  • Something you Have — hardware token, smart card, OTP device, phone (SMS/app).
  • Something you Are — biometrics: fingerprint, iris, retina, facial, voice.
Additional Factors
  • Somewhere you Are — geolocation, IP address restriction.
  • Something you Do — behavioral biometrics (typing rhythm, mouse movement).
MFA Rules
  • MFA = 2+ DIFFERENT factor types. Password + PIN = NOT MFA (both are "know").
  • Password + OTP app = MFA (know + have). ✅
Two passwords = Single Factor (both "something you know"). MFA requires factors from DIFFERENT categories. This is a common trick question.

📏 Access Control Models

+

Frameworks governing how access decisions are made and enforced.

Access ControlHigh Priority
Model Comparison
  • DAC — Discretionary. Owner controls access. Most flexible. Windows file permissions. Least secure.
  • MAC — Mandatory. System enforces via sensitivity labels. Most secure. Least flexible. Government.
  • RBAC — Role-Based. Access by job role. Most common enterprise model.
  • ABAC — Attribute-Based. Dynamic policies using user/resource/environment attributes. Most flexible.
  • Rule-Based — System rules (firewall ACLs, time-of-day restrictions).
DAC = most flexible (owner decides). MAC = most secure (system decides via labels). RBAC = most common in business. Know which model fits which scenario.

🌐 Federation & SSO

+

Technologies enabling single sign-on and cross-organization identity trust.

FederationCore
Key Protocols
  • SAML 2.0 — XML-based. Enterprise SSO between IdP and SP. Browser-based.
  • OAuth 2.0 — Authorization delegation framework. NOT authentication. "Login with Google."
  • OpenID Connect — Authentication layer on top of OAuth 2.0. Adds identity (who you are).
  • Kerberos — Ticket-based SSO. KDC issues TGT. Port 88. Active Directory uses it.

  • RADIUS — UDP 1812/1813. Encrypts only password. For network access.
  • TACACS+ — TCP 49. Encrypts entire payload. Separates A-A-A. Cisco networks.
OAuth = Authorization (what you can do). OpenID Connect = Authentication (who you are). They're often confused. SAML is the enterprise SSO standard.

👤 Identity Lifecycle

+

Managing identities from creation through modification to termination.

Lifecycle
Lifecycle Stages
  • Provisioning — creating accounts when someone joins.
  • Modification — updating access when role changes (transfers, promotions).
  • Review/Recertification — periodic access reviews to remove unnecessary permissions.
  • De-provisioning — disabling/deleting accounts on termination.
Key Risks
  • Orphan accounts — active accounts with no owner. Major security risk.
  • Privilege creep — accumulating excess permissions over time without removal.
  • Joiner-Mover-Leaver — process for managing identity events.
Privilege creep = biggest insider threat risk. Regular access recertification (e.g. quarterly reviews) is the key control. Managers must approve or revoke access periodically.

🧬 Biometrics

+

Using physical or behavioral characteristics for authentication.

BiometricsMemorize Metrics
Error Rates
  • FAR (Type II) — False Acceptance Rate. Unauthorized user ACCEPTED. Security risk!
  • FRR (Type I) — False Rejection Rate. Authorized user REJECTED. Usability issue.
  • CER/EER — Crossover Error Rate. Point where FAR = FRR. Lower = better system.
Sensitivity Trade-off
  • Increase sensitivity → FAR decreases, FRR increases (more secure, less usable).
  • Decrease sensitivity → FAR increases, FRR decreases (more usable, less secure).
FAR is the security concern (wrong person gets in). FRR is the usability concern (right person locked out). CER is used to compare biometric systems — choose the lowest CER.

🔑 Privileged Access Management

PAM2024 Focus
PAM Controls

  • Just-In-Time (JIT) — grant admin access only when needed, automatically revoke.
  • Session recording — all privileged sessions recorded for audit.
  • Least privilege — even admins should not have more than necessary.
Privileged accounts are the crown jewels. Compromising an admin account = game over. PAM is now considered a critical security control in every mature org.

📂 Directory Services

🔍 Vulnerability Assessment vs Pen Testing

+

Two complementary but distinct approaches to finding security weaknesses.

Testing TypesAlways Tested
Key Differences
  • Vulnerability Assessment — identifies and reports weaknesses. Non-destructive. Automated tools. No exploitation.
  • Penetration Test — actively exploits weaknesses. Simulates real attacker. Demonstrates actual impact.
  • Both require — written authorization and defined scope (Rules of Engagement) before starting.
VA = what vulnerabilities exist. PT = which ones are actually exploitable. A vuln scan finding doesn't mean it's exploitable — a pen test proves it. Both are needed.

🎭 Testing Knowledge Levels

TestingCore
Test Types

  • White Box (Crystal Box) — full knowledge (source code, architecture). Most thorough. Most efficient.
  • Gray Box — partial knowledge. Balanced approach. Most common in real engagements.
  • Double-blind — tester AND security staff unaware. Tests detection and response capabilities.

📊 Security Metrics & KPIs

+

Quantitative measures to track and communicate security program effectiveness.

MetricsMemorize
Key Metrics
  • MTTD — Mean Time To Detect. How fast incidents are found.
  • MTTR — Mean Time To Repair/Restore. How fast issues are fixed.
  • MTBF — Mean Time Between Failures. System reliability measure.
  • MTTF — Mean Time To Failure. For non-repairable components.
Formula
  • Availability = MTBF ÷ (MTBF + MTTR)
  • Higher MTBF + Lower MTTR = Higher Availability.
Five 9s availability = 99.999% = ~5.26 minutes downtime/year. To improve availability: increase MTBF (better hardware) or decrease MTTR (faster recovery).

🚨 Incident Response Process

+

The structured 6-phase process for detecting, containing, and recovering from incidents.

IRAlways Tested
NIST SP 800-61 Phases (PICERL)
  • 1. Preparation — IR plan, team, tools, training. Before anything happens.
  • 2. Identification/Detection — confirm incident, scope, severity.
  • 3. Containment — FIRST PRIORITY. Stop the spread. Short-term then long-term.
  • 4. Eradication — remove malware, close vulnerabilities, patch systems.
  • 5. Recovery — restore systems, verify normal operation, monitor.
  • 6. Lessons Learned — post-incident review, improve processes.
Containment is ALWAYS the first active step. Evidence collection is important but secondary to stopping the damage from spreading. "People In California Eat Real Lemons" = PICERL.

🔬 Digital Forensics

+

Scientifically collecting and analyzing digital evidence for legal proceedings.

ForensicsHigh Priority
Order of Volatility (collect first)
  • 1. CPU registers and cache (most volatile).
  • 2. RAM — running processes, network connections, encryption keys.
  • 3. Swap/page file.
  • 4. Disk storage.
  • 5. Remote/cloud logs (least volatile).
Key Principles
  • Chain of custody — document all evidence handling. Required for court admissibility.
  • Write blocker — prevents accidental modification of evidence media.
  • Forensic copy — bit-for-bit copy. Never work on originals.
  • Locard's Principle — every contact leaves a trace.
NEVER work on original evidence. Always make a forensic copy (hash-verified), work on the copy, and preserve the original. Breaking chain of custody = inadmissible evidence.

💾 Disaster Recovery

+

Technical plans and procedures to restore IT systems after a disaster.

DRCore
Recovery Sites
  • Hot Site — fully operational mirror. Minutes to failover. Most expensive.
  • Warm Site — partially configured. Hours to activate. Medium cost.
  • Cold Site — empty space + power. Days to weeks. Cheapest.
  • Reciprocal Agreement — two orgs agree to host each other. Low cost, low reliability.
Backup Types
  • Full — all data. Slowest backup, fastest restore (1 set).
  • Incremental — since last backup. Fastest backup, slowest restore (all sets).
  • Differential — since last FULL. Medium backup, medium restore (full + 1).
  • 3-2-1 Rule — 3 copies, 2 media types, 1 offsite.
RTO must be less than MTD or the business cannot survive. RPO determines backup frequency. If RPO = 4 hours, you must back up at least every 4 hours.

⚙️ Patch & Vulnerability Management

+

Patching
Patch Lifecycle
  • Identify — vulnerability scanning, vendor advisories, threat intel.
  • Prioritize — CVSS score, asset criticality, exploitability.
  • Test — validate patch in non-production environment.
  • Deploy — roll out using change management process.
  • Verify — confirm patch applied and vulnerability resolved.

🐛 Common Software Vulnerabilities

+

The most prevalent and dangerous software security weaknesses.

OWASPAlways Tested
OWASP Top 10 Highlights
  • Injection (A03) — SQL, XSS, command injection. Input treated as code. Fix: parameterized queries.
  • Broken Access Control (A01) — users can act beyond their permissions. Fix: deny by default.
  • Cryptographic Failures (A02) — weak/no encryption. Fix: TLS, strong algorithms.
  • Security Misconfiguration (A05) — default credentials, open cloud buckets. Fix: hardening.
  • SSRF (A10) — server-side request forgery. Server makes requests attacker controls.
Injection is the classic OWASP vulnerability. SQL injection is the most common. Parameterized queries (prepared statements) are the primary defense. Input validation is secondary.

💻 Secure Coding Practices

+

Developer-level security practices that prevent common vulnerabilities.

CodingHigh Priority
Core Practices
  • Input validation — whitelist (allow known good) preferred over blacklist.
  • Parameterized queries — prevent SQL injection. Never concatenate user input into SQL.
  • Output encoding — encode output to prevent XSS.
  • Error handling — generic error messages. Don't expose stack traces or system details.
  • Least privilege for DB — app's DB account should only have needed permissions.
Never trust user input. Ever. Validate on the server side — client-side validation is easily bypassed. Whitelist (allow only known good) is always stronger than blacklist.

Memory Aid

CISSP Cheat Sheet

Critical concepts, formulas, mnemonics, and "always-tested" facts. Based on Sybex study guide key takeaways.

Risk Quantification

SLE = Asset Value × Exposure Factor (EF)
ALE = SLE × ARO (Annual Rate of Occurrence)
Countermeasure Value = ALE(before) − ALE(after) − Annual Cost
Risk = Threat × Vulnerability × Asset Value
EF: % of asset destroyed in one incident (0–1)
ARO: How many times per year event occurs (e.g., 0.5 = once every 2 yrs)
SLE Example: $1M server × 40% EF = $400K SLE
ALE Example: $400K × 0.5 = $200K/year

Availability & Recovery Metrics

Availability = MTBF / (MTBF + MTTR)
MTBF = Mean Time Between Failures
MTTR = Mean Time To Repair/Restore
MTTF = Mean Time To Failure (non-repairable)
RTO: How quickly must system be restored (management decision)
RPO: How much data loss is acceptable (backup frequency determines)
MTD: Maximum Tolerable Downtime — business limit. RTO must be less than MTD!
WRT: Work Recovery Time = MTD − RTO

Biometric Error Rates

FAR = False Acceptance Rate (Type II Error)
FRR = False Rejection Rate (Type I Error)
CER / EER = Crossover Error Rate (lower = better)
FAR: Unauthorized user incorrectly accepted — SECURITY risk
FRR: Authorized user incorrectly rejected — USABILITY issue
CER: Point where FAR = FRR. Used to compare biometric systems.

Backup Math

Full: Backup all data. Restore: 1 tape
Differential: Changes since last FULL. Restore: Full + latest diff
Incremental: Changes since last backup. Restore: Full + all incrementals
Incremental: Faster backup, slower restore
Differential: Slower backup, faster restore
3-2-1 Rule: 3 copies, 2 different media, 1 offsite

Bell-LaPadula (Confidentiality)

No Read Up (NRU): Simple Security Property — can't read above clearance
No Write Down (NWD): Star Property (*) — can't write below your level
Strong Star: Read and write only at same level
Goal: Prevent classified info from flowing to lower levels. Military model.

Biba Model (Integrity)

No Read Down (NRD): Simple Integrity Axiom — don't read low-integrity data
No Write Up (NWU): Star Integrity Property — don't write to higher integrity
Opposite of Bell-LaPadula. Goal: Prevent data corruption by low-integrity sources.
Biba = Integrity. Bell-LaPadula = Confidentiality. Remember: B comes before BL alphabetically, I comes before C.

Clark-Wilson (Integrity)

CDI: Constrained Data Item — data that must maintain integrity
UDI: Unconstrained Data Item — user input/external data
TP: Transformation Procedure — only authorized way to change CDI
IVP: Integrity Verification Procedure — checks CDI validity
Used in commercial environments. Separation of duties built in.

Other Key Models

Brewer-Nash (Chinese Wall): Prevents conflict of interest — access to data changes based on what you've accessed
Graham-Denning: How subjects and objects are created/deleted; how rights are assigned
Take-Grant: Uses graph to show how rights are passed between subjects
Lattice: Uses upper/lower bounds for access control in MAC environments
State Machine: Must be secure in all possible states. Foundation for many other models.

Access Control Quick Reference

DAC: Owner controls access (Windows ACLs, Unix permissions) — most flexible
MAC: System enforces via labels — most secure, least flexible (government)
RBAC: Access by job role — most common in enterprise
ABAC: Dynamic policies based on attributes (user, resource, environment)
Rule-Based: System-enforced rules (firewall ACLs, time-of-day restrictions)

Symmetric Algorithms

AES: 128/192/256-bit keys. Block cipher (128-bit blocks). Current standard. FIPS 197.
3DES: 112-168 bit effective. Slow. Being phased out. 3 rounds of DES.
Blowfish/Twofish: Variable key up to 448-bit. Twofish finalist for AES selection.
RC4: Stream cipher. Broken. Used in WEP (which is why WEP is broken).
DES: 56-bit. Broken. Do not use.

Asymmetric Algorithms

RSA: 1024+ bits (2048+ recommended). Based on prime factorization. Used for key exchange + signatures.
ECC: Smaller key = same security as RSA. 256-bit ECC ≈ 3072-bit RSA. Used in mobile/IoT.
Diffie-Hellman: Key EXCHANGE only (not encryption). Enables shared secret over public channel.
El Gamal: Based on DH. Used for encryption and signatures. Doubles message size.
DSA: Digital Signature Algorithm. Signatures only, NOT encryption.

Hash Functions

MD5: 128-bit. BROKEN (collision attacks). Never use for security.
SHA-1: 160-bit. WEAK (collision demonstrated). Deprecated.
SHA-256: 256-bit. Current standard. Part of SHA-2 family.
SHA-3: Keccak algorithm. Different internal structure. Quantum-resistant candidate.
HMAC: Hash + secret key = integrity AND authentication (not just integrity).

Digital Signatures & PKI

Signing: Sender encrypts hash with PRIVATE key → proves authenticity
Verification: Receiver decrypts with sender's PUBLIC key → verifies signature
Encryption: Encrypt with recipient's PUBLIC key; decrypt with PRIVATE key
CRL: Certificate Revocation List — periodic batch revocation check
OCSP: Online Certificate Status Protocol — real-time revocation check
Key Escrow: Copy of private key held by third party for recovery

Cipher Modes

ECB: Electronic Codebook — same block = same ciphertext. INSECURE, never use.
CBC: Cipher Block Chaining — uses IV, each block depends on previous. Parallelization on decrypt only.
CTR: Counter mode — converts block cipher to stream cipher. Allows parallelization.
GCM: Galois/Counter Mode — provides encryption + authentication. Used in TLS 1.3.

Critical Port Numbers

20/21 FTP — Use SFTP (22) or FTPS instead
22 SSH/SFTP/SCP — Secure shell and file transfer
23 Telnet — INSECURE, plaintext. Replace with SSH.
25 SMTP / 587 SMTP-TLS — Email sending
53 DNS — UDP (queries), TCP (zone transfers). Use DNSSEC.
80 HTTP / 443 HTTPS — Web traffic
389 LDAP / 636 LDAPS — Directory services
443 HTTPS / TLS — Encrypted web
1433 MSSQL / 3306 MySQL / 5432 PostgreSQL
3389 RDP — Remote Desktop. High-risk port. Always restrict.

Network Security Protocols Hierarchy

TLS 1.3: Current gold standard. Eliminated weak ciphers. 0-RTT handshake.
IPSec: AH (auth only, no encrypt) + ESP (auth + encrypt). Tunnel vs Transport mode.
DNSSEC: Signs DNS records. Prevents DNS spoofing. Does NOT encrypt DNS.
SRTP: Secure Real-time Transport Protocol. Encrypts VoIP.
S/MIME: Email encryption + signing. Uses X.509 certs. vs PGP (web of trust).

Wireless Protocol Security

WEP: BROKEN. RC4 misuse + weak IV. Never use.
WPA: TKIP (interim fix). Still weak. Don't use.
WPA2: AES/CCMP. Strong. PSK (personal) vs Enterprise (802.1X/RADIUS).
WPA3: SAE replaces PSK. Dragonfly handshake. Forward secrecy. Current standard.
802.1X: Port-based NAC. Supplicant → Authenticator → Auth Server (RADIUS).

Authentication Protocols

PAP: Plaintext passwords. INSECURE.
CHAP: Challenge-Handshake. 3-way. Passwords never sent.
Kerberos: Ticket-based SSO. KDC issues TGT. Port 88. Vulnerable to Pass-the-Ticket.
RADIUS: AAA for network access. UDP 1812/1813. Encrypts only password field.
TACACS+: Cisco AAA. TCP 49. Encrypts entire payload. Separates A-A-A.
SAML: XML-based. Enterprise SSO/Federation. IdP issues assertions.

Security Model Memory Tricks

Bell-LaPadula = "No Read Up, No Write Down" → Confidentiality. Keep secrets SECRET at the top. "BLP = Be Leaking Prevention"
Biba = "No Read Down, No Write Up" → Integrity. Don't get corrupted by low quality. "Biba = Be Integrity, Bruh, Always"
FAR vs FRR: "FAR = False Acceptance = Risk" vs "FRR = False Rejection = annoyance"

Backup Types — Speed vs Storage

FASTEST BACKUP → Incremental (smallest)
SLOWEST BACKUP → Full (largest)
FASTEST RESTORE → Full (only 1 tape)
SLOWEST RESTORE → Incremental (many tapes)
Differential is the middle ground: medium backup, medium restore (Full + 1 Diff tape)

Recovery Site Types

Hot Site → Ready NOW, expensive 💰💰💰
Warm Site → Hours/days, medium cost 💰💰
Cold Site → Days/weeks, cheap 💰
