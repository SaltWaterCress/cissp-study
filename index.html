Study Guide

All 8 Domains — Deep Dive

Based on the Sybex CISSP Study Guide and official ISC² exam outline. Select a domain to study.

D1

Security & Risk Management

The largest domain. Covers foundational security concepts, risk frameworks, legal/regulatory compliance, personnel security, and business continuity. Think like a CISO managing organizational risk — not a technician.

▲ 16% of Exam (Updated 2024)

🔒 CIA Triad & Security Concepts

Confidentiality (prevent unauthorized disclosure), Integrity (prevent unauthorized modification), Availability (ensure authorized access). Also: Authenticity, Non-repudiation, Privacy.

FoundationalAlways Tested

⚖️ Security Governance

Aligning security with business strategy. Policies → Standards → Guidelines → Procedures hierarchy. Security function must support mission/goals. Board-level oversight, due care vs due diligence.

ManagementHigh Priority

📊 Risk Management

Risk = Threat × Vulnerability × Asset Value. Risk responses: Accept, Transfer, Avoid, Mitigate. Qualitative (subjective) vs Quantitative (ALE=ARO×SLE). Risk appetite vs risk tolerance.

QuantitativeCore Topic

🧮 Risk Calculations (Quantitative)

SLE = Asset Value × EF. ARO = times per year. ALE = SLE × ARO. Countermeasure value: ALE(before) – ALE(after) – Annual Cost. Essential formulas for the exam.

MathMemorize

📜 Legal, Regulations & Compliance

GDPR, HIPAA, SOX, PCI-DSS, FERPA. Types of laws: Criminal, Civil, Administrative. Intellectual property: Patent, Copyright, Trademark, Trade Secret. Jurisdiction and transborder data flow.

Regulatory

🏗️ Security Frameworks

NIST CSF (Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover), ISO 27001/27002, COBIT, ITIL, TOGAF. Know when to apply each. Frameworks provide structure; standards are mandatory.

FrameworkHigh Priority

👥 Personnel Security

Background checks, NDA, separation of duties, least privilege, mandatory vacation, job rotation. Termination procedures (hostile vs friendly). Employee monitoring and privacy issues.

HROperational

🏢 Business Continuity (BCP)

BCP = long-term recovery. BIA identifies critical functions, MTD (Maximum Tolerable Downtime), RTO (Recovery Time Objective), RPO (Recovery Point Objective). Difference: BCP vs DRP.

BCP/DRHigh Priority

🔑 Security Awareness Training

Training vs Education vs Awareness. Social engineering awareness (phishing, pretexting, vishing). Security culture vs compliance culture. Role-based training requirements.

People

🤝 Third-Party Risk

Vendor due diligence, SLAs, right-to-audit clauses, supply chain risk. SCRM (Supply Chain Risk Management). On-site assessments, document reviews, process/policy review.

Supply Chain2024 Added

🔬 Threat Intelligence

Threat actors: Nation-state, hacktivists, insiders, organized crime. STIX/TAXII for sharing. APT characteristics. Threat modeling (STRIDE, PASTA, DREAD). Attack trees.

Threat

🛡️ Ethics

ISC² Code of Ethics: Protect society → Act honorably → Provide competent service → Advance the profession. Ethical reasoning in gray-area scenarios. Computer ethics frameworks.

EthicsAlways Tested
D2

Asset Security

Focuses on classifying information and supporting assets, determining ownership, handling data throughout its lifecycle, and applying appropriate protection measures. Resource protection and data management are central themes.

10% of Exam

🏷️ Data Classification

Government: Top Secret → Secret → Confidential → Unclassified. Commercial: Confidential/Proprietary → Private → Sensitive → Public. Owner sets classification; custodian implements controls.

ClassificationCore

👤 Data Ownership

Data Owner: accountable for classification and protection decisions. Data Custodian: implements controls (IT dept). Data Processor (GDPR): processes on behalf of controller. User: accesses per policy.

RolesAlways Tested

♻️ Data Lifecycle

Create → Store → Use → Share → Archive → Destroy. Data remanence concerns during destruction. NIST SP 800-88: Clear, Purge, Destroy. Degaussing for magnetic media.

Lifecycle

🔒 Data States

Data at Rest: stored data (encryption at rest, access controls). Data in Transit: moving across networks (TLS, VPN). Data in Use/Processing: in RAM/CPU (DRM, access controls, trusted execution).

Data StatesCore

🗑️ Data Retention & Destruction

Retention policies based on legal/business requirements. Overwriting (1× to 7×), degaussing (magnetic), physical destruction (shredding, incineration). Crypto-shredding for cloud.

Destruction

🔐 Privacy Protection

PII (Personally Identifiable Information) vs PHI (Protected Health Info). Privacy by design, data minimization, purpose limitation. GDPR right to erasure, data portability, consent requirements.

PrivacyGDPR

📦 Asset Management

Hardware/software inventory, asset valuation. Tangible vs intangible assets. Configuration Management Database (CMDB). Lifecycle management from procurement to disposal.

Assets

☁️ Cloud Data Security

Data sovereignty concerns, cloud provider shared responsibility model. Data Loss Prevention (DLP) in cloud. CASB (Cloud Access Security Broker) for visibility and control.

Cloud
D3

Security Architecture & Engineering

Covers secure design principles, security models, cryptography, physical security, and vulnerability assessment across computing architectures. The most technically deep domain.

13% of Exam

🏛️ Secure Design Principles

Least Privilege, Defense in Depth, Fail Secure/Safe, Economy of Mechanism, Complete Mediation, Open Design, Separation of Privilege, Least Common Mechanism, Psychological Acceptability, Zero Trust.

PrinciplesCore

🔐 Security Models

Bell-LaPadula (confidentiality: no read up, no write down). Biba (integrity: no read down, no write up). Clark-Wilson (integrity via transactions). Brewer-Nash (Chinese Wall — conflict of interest).

ModelsAlways Tested

🖥️ Security Evaluation Criteria

Common Criteria (ISO 15408): EAL1–EAL7 levels. Protection Profile vs Security Target. TCSEC (Orange Book): D, C1, C2, B1, B2, B3, A1. ITSEC European equivalent.

Evaluation

🔑 Cryptography Fundamentals

Symmetric (AES, 3DES, Blowfish) — fast, same key. Asymmetric (RSA, ECC, Diffie-Hellman) — key pairs, slow. Hybrid: symmetric for data, asymmetric for key exchange. Hash: MD5(broken), SHA-1(weak), SHA-256/512.

CryptoCore

📜 PKI & Digital Certificates

CA (Certificate Authority) hierarchy. X.509 certificates. CRL (Certificate Revocation List) vs OCSP (Online Certificate Status Protocol). Key escrow, key recovery. Trust models: hierarchical, web of trust, bridge.

PKIHigh Priority

🏗️ System Security Architecture

TCB (Trusted Computing Base), security perimeter, reference monitor, security kernel. Modes: Dedicated, System High, Compartmented, Multi-level. Ring architecture (0=kernel → 3=user).

Architecture

🛡️ Physical Security

Crime Prevention Through Environmental Design (CPTED). Layers: perimeter, outer, inner, target. Controls: fences, bollards, mantraps, biometrics, guards. Data center: raised floors, fire suppression (FM-200).

Physical

☁️ Cloud Security Architecture

IaaS/PaaS/SaaS responsibility models. Virtualization security (hypervisor attacks, VM escape). Container security (Docker, Kubernetes). Serverless security. Zero Trust Architecture (ZTA).

Cloud2024 Focus

🔌 IoT & Embedded Systems

Constrained devices, firmware security, OTA updates. ICS/SCADA security (Purdue Model). CAN bus vulnerabilities. Embedded system isolation, air gaps. RTOS security considerations.

IoTICS/SCADA

⚠️ Common Vulnerabilities

Buffer overflow, race conditions, covert channels (storage/timing), TOC/TOU attacks, rootkits, firmware exploits. Vulnerability in cryptographic implementations (side-channel attacks).

Vulnerabilities
D4

Communication & Network Security

Design and protect network architecture, understand protocols, implement secure communications, and protect against network attacks. Know the OSI model cold and which security measures apply at each layer.

13% of Exam

🌐 OSI & TCP/IP Models

OSI 7 layers: Physical, Data Link, Network, Transport, Session, Presentation, Application. TCP/IP: Network Access, Internet, Transport, Application. Know protocols at each layer (ARP=2, IP=3, TCP/UDP=4, HTTP=7).

ModelsAlways Tested

🔒 Network Security Protocols

TLS 1.3 (replaces SSL), IPSec (AH + ESP), SSH (replaces Telnet/rsh), SFTP/SCP, HTTPS, DNSSEC, S/MIME for email. Know which protocols are secure vs deprecated (SSLv3, TLS 1.0/1.1 deprecated).

ProtocolsCore

🏗️ Network Architecture

DMZ (3-legged firewall), segmentation, VLANs, microsegmentation. East-west vs north-south traffic. Zero Trust Network Access (ZTNA). Software Defined Networking (SDN) and NFV.

ArchitectureHigh Priority

🔥 Firewalls & Filtering

Packet filtering (L3/L4), stateful inspection (tracks connections), application-layer proxy (L7). NGFW (deep packet inspection). WAF (Web Application Firewall). Implicit deny vs explicit permit.

Firewalls

📡 Wireless Security

WEP (broken) → WPA → WPA2 (AES/CCMP) → WPA3. TKIP (deprecated). Evil twin attacks, war driving, rogue APs. 802.1X (EAP/RADIUS) for enterprise auth. Bluetooth, NFC, Zigbee risks.

WirelessCore

🔑 VPN Technologies

Site-to-site vs remote access VPN. IPSec (tunnel vs transport mode). SSL/TLS VPN. Split tunneling risks. Always-on VPN. SD-WAN security implications. Zero Trust vs traditional VPN.

VPN

🛡️ IDS/IPS

IDS (detects only) vs IPS (blocks). NIDS (network) vs HIDS (host). Signature-based vs anomaly-based vs behavioral. False positives vs false negatives trade-offs. Inline vs passive deployment.

IDS/IPS

⚡ Network Attacks

DoS/DDoS (volumetric, protocol, application), ARP poisoning, DNS hijacking, BGP hijacking, MITM, session hijacking, replay attacks, sniffing. Know mitigation for each attack type.

Attacks

📞 Voice & Multimedia Security

VoIP security: SRTP (encrypted RTP), SIP vulnerabilities. VLAN separation for voice traffic. Vishing attacks. Unified communications security. Video conferencing risks.

VoIP
D5

Identity & Access Management (IAM)

Controls how users, devices, and services are identified, authenticated, and granted access. The first line of defense. Understand the full IAM lifecycle and federation technologies.

13% of Exam

🪪 Identification vs Authentication vs Authorization

Identification: claiming identity (username). Authentication: proving identity (password/MFA). Authorization: what you're allowed to do (ACL, roles). Accountability: logging actions (audit trail).

Core ConceptsAlways Tested

🔐 Authentication Factors

Something you Know (password, PIN, KBA). Something you Have (token, smart card, OTP). Something you Are (biometrics). Somewhere you Are (location). MFA = 2+ different factors. 2FA ≠ always MFA.

AuthenticationCore

📏 Access Control Models

DAC (Discretionary — owner controls), MAC (Mandatory — labels, government), RBAC (Role-Based — business roles), ABAC (Attribute-Based — dynamic policies), Rule-Based. RBAC most common in enterprise.

Access ControlHigh Priority

🌐 Federation & SSO

SAML 2.0 (XML-based, enterprise SSO). OAuth 2.0 (authorization delegation). OpenID Connect (authentication on top of OAuth). Kerberos (tickets, KDC). RADIUS/TACACS+ for network auth.

FederationCore

👤 Identity Lifecycle

👤 Identity Lifecycle

Provisioning (creating accounts), modification (role changes), review (recertification), de-provisioning (termination). Orphan accounts risk. Joiner-Mover-Leaver process. Role mining and SoD.

Lifecycle

🧬 Biometrics

FRR (False Rejection Rate) vs FAR (False Acceptance Rate). CER/EER (Crossover Error Rate) — lower is better. Types: fingerprint, iris, retina, voice, facial, gait. Enrollment and template storage risks.

BiometricsMemorize Metrics

🔑 Privileged Access Management

PAM solutions for admin accounts. Just-In-Time (JIT) access. Privileged Identity Management (PIM). Session recording for privileged accounts. Principle of least privilege enforcement.

PAM2024 Focus

📂 Directory Services

LDAP (port 389, 636 for LDAPS), Active Directory structure (domains, forests, OUs). Group Policy. Azure AD/Entra ID for cloud. Attribute-based access control from directory attributes.

MetricsMemorize

📝 Log Management & SIEM

SIEM

🧪 Software Testing

Unit → Integration → System → Acceptance testing. Regression testing after patches. Fuzzing (mutation-based, generation-based). Interface testing. API security testing. Test environments vs production.

Software Testing

🔄 Continuous Monitoring

Continuous2024 Focus

CISSP Cheat Sheet

Critical concepts, formulas, mnemonics, and "always-tested" facts. Based on Sybex study guide key takeaways.

Risk Quantification

SLE = Asset Value × Exposure Factor (EF)
ALE = SLE × ARO (Annual Rate of Occurrence)
Countermeasure Value = ALE(before) − ALE(after) − Annual Cost
Risk = Threat × Vulnerability × Asset Value
EF: % of asset destroyed in one incident (0–1)
ARO: How many times per year event occurs (e.g., 0.5 = once every 2 yrs)
SLE Example: $1M server × 40% EF = $400K SLE
ALE Example: $400K × 0.5 = $200K/year

Availability & Recovery Metrics

Availability = MTBF / (MTBF + MTTR)
MTBF = Mean Time Between Failures
MTTR = Mean Time To Repair/Restore
MTTF = Mean Time To Failure (non-repairable)
RTO: How quickly must system be restored (management decision)
RPO: How much data loss is acceptable (backup frequency determines)
MTD: Maximum Tolerable Downtime — business limit. RTO must be less than MTD!
WRT: Work Recovery Time = MTD − RTO

Biometric Error Rates

FAR = False Acceptance Rate (Type II Error)
FRR = False Rejection Rate (Type I Error)
CER / EER = Crossover Error Rate (lower = better)
FAR: Unauthorized user incorrectly accepted — SECURITY risk
FRR: Authorized user incorrectly rejected — USABILITY issue
CER: Point where FAR = FRR. Used to compare biometric systems.

Backup Math

Full: Backup all data. Restore: 1 tape
Differential: Changes since last FULL. Restore: Full + latest diff
Incremental: Changes since last backup. Restore: Full + all incrementals
Incremental: Faster backup, slower restore
Differential: Slower backup, faster restore
3-2-1 Rule: 3 copies, 2 different media, 1 offsite

Bell-LaPadula (Confidentiality)

No Read Up (NRU): Simple Security Property — can't read above clearance
No Write Down (NWD): Star Property (*) — can't write below your level
Strong Star: Read and write only at same level
Goal: Prevent classified info from flowing to lower levels. Military model.

Biba Model (Integrity)

No Read Down (NRD): Simple Integrity Axiom — don't read low-integrity data
No Write Up (NWU): Star Integrity Property — don't write to higher integrity
Opposite of Bell-LaPadula. Goal: Prevent data corruption by low-integrity sources.
Biba = Integrity. Bell-LaPadula = Confidentiality. Remember: B comes before BL alphabetically, I comes before C.

Clark-Wilson (Integrity)

CDI: Constrained Data Item — data that must maintain integrity
UDI: Unconstrained Data Item — user input/external data
TP: Transformation Procedure — only authorized way to change CDI
IVP: Integrity Verification Procedure — checks CDI validity
Used in commercial environments. Separation of duties built in.

Other Key Models

Brewer-Nash (Chinese Wall): Prevents conflict of interest — access to data changes based on what you've accessed
Graham-Denning: How subjects and objects are created/deleted; how rights are assigned
Take-Grant: Uses graph to show how rights are passed between subjects
Lattice: Uses upper/lower bounds for access control in MAC environments
State Machine: Must be secure in all possible states. Foundation for many other models.

Access Control Quick Reference

DAC: Owner controls access (Windows ACLs, Unix permissions) — most flexible
MAC: System enforces via labels — most secure, least flexible (government)
RBAC: Access by job role — most common in enterprise
ABAC: Dynamic policies based on attributes (user, resource, environment)
Rule-Based: System-enforced rules (firewall ACLs, time-of-day restrictions)

Symmetric Algorithms

AES: 128/192/256-bit keys. Block cipher (128-bit blocks). Current standard. FIPS 197.
3DES: 112-168 bit effective. Slow. Being phased out. 3 rounds of DES.
Blowfish/Twofish: Variable key up to 448-bit. Twofish finalist for AES selection.
RC4: Stream cipher. Broken. Used in WEP (which is why WEP is broken).
DES: 56-bit. Broken. Do not use.

Asymmetric Algorithms

RSA: 1024+ bits (2048+ recommended). Based on prime factorization. Used for key exchange + signatures.
ECC: Smaller key = same security as RSA. 256-bit ECC ≈ 3072-bit RSA. Used in mobile/IoT.
Diffie-Hellman: Key EXCHANGE only (not encryption). Enables shared secret over public channel.
El Gamal: Based on DH. Used for encryption and signatures. Doubles message size.
DSA: Digital Signature Algorithm. Signatures only, NOT encryption.

Hash Functions

MD5: 128-bit. BROKEN (collision attacks). Never use for security.
SHA-1: 160-bit. WEAK (collision demonstrated). Deprecated.
SHA-256: 256-bit. Current standard. Part of SHA-2 family.
SHA-3: Keccak algorithm. Different internal structure. Quantum-resistant candidate.
HMAC: Hash + secret key = integrity AND authentication (not just integrity).

Digital Signatures & PKI

Signing: Sender encrypts hash with PRIVATE key → proves authenticity
Verification: Receiver decrypts with sender's PUBLIC key → verifies signature
Encryption: Encrypt with recipient's PUBLIC key; decrypt with PRIVATE key
CRL: Certificate Revocation List — periodic batch revocation check
OCSP: Online Certificate Status Protocol — real-time revocation check
Key Escrow: Copy of private key held by third party for recovery

Cipher Modes

ECB: Electronic Codebook — same block = same ciphertext. INSECURE, never use.
CBC: Cipher Block Chaining — uses IV, each block depends on previous. Parallelization on decrypt only.
CTR: Counter mode — converts block cipher to stream cipher. Allows parallelization.
GCM: Galois/Counter Mode — provides encryption + authentication. Used in TLS 1.3.

Critical Port Numbers

20/21 FTP — Use SFTP (22) or FTPS instead
22 SSH/SFTP/SCP — Secure shell and file transfer
23 Telnet — INSECURE, plaintext. Replace with SSH.
25 SMTP / 587 SMTP-TLS — Email sending
53 DNS — UDP (queries), TCP (zone transfers). Use DNSSEC.
80 HTTP / 443 HTTPS — Web traffic
389 LDAP / 636 LDAPS — Directory services
443 HTTPS / TLS — Encrypted web
1433 MSSQL / 3306 MySQL / 5432 PostgreSQL
3389 RDP — Remote Desktop. High-risk port. Always restrict.

Network Security Protocols Hierarchy

TLS 1.3: Current gold standard. Eliminated weak ciphers. 0-RTT handshake.
IPSec: AH (auth only, no encrypt) + ESP (auth + encrypt). Tunnel vs Transport mode.
DNSSEC: Signs DNS records. Prevents DNS spoofing. Does NOT encrypt DNS.
SRTP: Secure Real-time Transport Protocol. Encrypts VoIP.
S/MIME: Email encryption + signing. Uses X.509 certs. vs PGP (web of trust).

Wireless Protocol Security

WEP: BROKEN. RC4 misuse + weak IV. Never use.
WPA: TKIP (interim fix). Still weak. Don't use.
WPA2: AES/CCMP. Strong. PSK (personal) vs Enterprise (802.1X/RADIUS).
WPA3: SAE replaces PSK. Dragonfly handshake. Forward secrecy. Current standard.
802.1X: Port-based NAC. Supplicant → Authenticator → Auth Server (RADIUS).

Authentication Protocols

PAP: Plaintext passwords. INSECURE.
CHAP: Challenge-Handshake. 3-way. Passwords never sent.
Kerberos: Ticket-based SSO. KDC issues TGT. Port 88. Vulnerable to Pass-the-Ticket.
RADIUS: AAA for network access. UDP 1812/1813. Encrypts only password field.
TACACS+: Cisco AAA. TCP 49. Encrypts entire payload. Separates A-A-A.
SAML: XML-based. Enterprise SSO/Federation. IdP issues assertions.

(Application, Presentation, Session, Transport, Network, Data Link, Physical)

Security Model Memory Tricks

Bell-LaPadula = "No Read Up, No Write Down" → Confidentiality. Keep secrets SECRET at the top. "BLP = Be Leaking Prevention"
Biba = "No Read Down, No Write Up" → Integrity. Don't get corrupted by low quality. "Biba = Be Integrity, Bruh, Always"
FAR vs FRR: "FAR = False Acceptance = Risk" vs "FRR = False Rejection = annoyance"

Backup Types — Speed vs Storage

FASTEST BACKUP → Incremental (smallest)
SLOWEST BACKUP → Full (largest)
FASTEST RESTORE → Full (only 1 tape)
SLOWEST RESTORE → Incremental (many tapes)
Differential is the middle ground: medium backup, medium restore (Full + 1 Diff tape)

Recovery Site Types

Hot Site → Ready NOW, expensive 💰💰💰
Warm Site → Hours/days, medium cost 💰💰
Cold Site → Days/weeks, cheap 💰

ISC² Code of Ethics Priority Order

1. Protect SOCIETY, common good
2. Act HONORABLY, honestly, justly
3. Provide COMPETENT service
4. Advance the PROFESSION
"Society before Self" — when in doubt, choose the option that protects the public
EXAM TIP: If a question asks about ethics, society/public good always wins over employer interest

US Privacy & Security Laws

HIPAA: Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act — protects PHI (Protected Health Information)
GLBA: Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act — financial institutions, NPI (Non-Public Personal Info)
SOX: Sarbanes-Oxley — public companies, financial reporting integrity, CEO/CFO accountability
COPPA: Children's Online Privacy Protection Act — under 13 year olds, parental consent
FERPA: Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act — student education records
FISMA: Federal Information Security Modernization Act — US federal agencies

International Regulations

GDPR: EU General Data Protection Regulation. 72-hr breach notification. Right to erasure. Data minimization. Fines up to 4% global revenue.
PIPEDA: Canadian privacy law for commercial activities
Privacy Shield: EU-US data transfer framework (invalidated, replaced by Data Privacy Framework)
APEC CBPR: Asia-Pacific cross-border privacy rules

Security Standards

PCI-DSS: Payment Card Industry DSS — cardholder data. 12 requirements. Any org processing cards.
ISO 27001: ISMS certification standard (requirements)
ISO 27002: Code of practice (implementation guidance) for 27001
NIST SP 800-53: Security controls for federal systems
NIST CSF: Cybersecurity Framework — Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover
SOC 2 Type II: Service org controls audit — Trust Service Criteria (security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, privacy)

Types of Laws

Criminal Law: Government prosecutes, "beyond reasonable doubt", imprisonment possible
Civil (Tort) Law: Private disputes, "preponderance of evidence", monetary damages
Administrative/Regulatory: Government agencies, compliance fines, licenses
Due Care: Doing what a reasonable person would do (minimum standard)
Due Diligence: Researching and planning — ongoing effort to maintain due care
